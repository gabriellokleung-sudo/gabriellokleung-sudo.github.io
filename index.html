Being part of the commissioning phase of the clinical manufacturing plant was one of the most exciting and challenging experiences of my career. There were plenty of long days and unexpected issues, from debugging automation systems to coordinating with different teams under tight deadlines. But that’s what made it so rewarding. I really enjoyed the fast pace and problem-solving environment, and it was incredible to see everything come together from the ground up. It gave me a real sense of pride and showed me how critical our work is to getting life-changing therapies to patients.

The birthplace of biotechnology

Working at Genentech has been an incredibly rewarding experience. I’ve been continually inspired by the dedication and expertise of my colleagues. On-call support

Addressing these issues could be stressful at times, as it took careful analysis to identify the root cause of the problem. Despite the challenges, the process was also exciting—it tested my problem-solving skills and kept me on my toes. Successfully resolving an issue always brought a sense of fulfillment, knowing I had contributed to keeping systems running smoothly.

Robotic Aseptic Filling

As a control systems engineer, I was responsible for ensuring the overall functionality and performance of our aseptic robotic fillers. After a system was assembled by the manufacturing team, it was handed over to my team. I started the integration process from the ground up—setting up computers, PLCs, and fluidic subsystems, as well as teaching robotic arms positions and configuring vision system algorithms. Once a system was functional, I executed GMP commissioning, FAT, and SAT to validate performance quality. Additionally, I provided technical service to customers when they encountered issues with our systems.

Integration

When a machine is manufactured, it is not yet considered functional. My integration work makes sure every component of the system, hardware and software, works together as intended.

Troubleshoot

Issues can build up throughout the manufacturing process, for many reasons. These issues are often not apparent until a control systems engineer starts integrating the machine.

Validation

After a system is fully integrated, we work together with process engineers to ensure our products are validated according to GMP standards, including commissioning, FAT, SAT and IQ/OQ..

Smart Vending Machine and more

Drinkfill sets out to change what a vending machine can be. When it comes to vending machines in North America, the machine most likely to come to your mind would probably be snack dispensers or pop dispensers that have been around since the 80s. We believe vending machines can be so much more. With Drinkfill, our goal is to:

  • Promote zero plastic bottle waste
  • Provide premium quality freshly made and handcrafted beverages
  • Create fun and interactive customer experience
  • Perform smart data analysis and management

My role

I’m the lead mechatronics engineer on Drinkfill. My responsibility is to bridge the software and mechanical designs. This includes designing the overall circuitry for power, fluidic components, as well as programming the controller that controls the mechanism of the machine. Together with the team, I’ve taken a major role in designing, prototyping and iterating Drinkfill to be the product we envision it to be. Now, we are focusing on pushing it to a production grade product.

Shown here are photos of Drinkfill Mini, a proof-of-concept prototype that was produced and beta-launched. The purpose of this product was to help us collect data for engineering improvements, usability, customer behavior, etc. We were able to deploy a total of 5 Drinkfill Minis at different locations in Greater Vancouver Region, including UBC, Langara College, HiVE shared office and other office environments.

Wearable Airbag

This is a university prototyping project of a wearable device containing sensor technology that can inflate an airbag to reduce injury due to falling. It utilizes a gyro-accelerometer sensor to monitor the user's motion and detect falls.

The fundamental goal of this project is to create a proof-of-concept model to justify the design approach.

Type
University capstone
Sensing
Gyro-accelerometer on an Arduino UNO
Airbag
About 3 L of compressed CO2, inflates in about 600 ms
Goal
A proof of concept for preventing fall injuries

Understanding the needs

One out of five falls causes a serious injury, including broken wrist, arm, ankle and hip fractures.

More than 95% of hip fractures are caused by falling, usually by falling sideways.

Fall injuries cost more than US$50 billion in medical costs in 2015.

Proposed Design Solution

01 | To design and prototype an active airbag system to reduce fall injuries

02 | The system must not interfere with the user’s daily activities

03 | The system will be easily integrated into normal daily clothing and be concealed within

Intended users include athletes, elderly people, rehabilitation patients or other individuals that require fall injuries prevention

When designing the system, several attributes were considered and ranked to ensure our product can meet our goals and customers’ satisfaction. The most important factor is safety, as our customers will wear it on a daily basis. Reliability is our second most important attribute because our customers rely on our system to dampen the fall impact. The following attributes are related to material use, size, and user-friendliness bridging into the final marketable product.

Airbag module specifications

  • Material: Nylon & PEVA (polyethylene vinyl acetate)
  • Volume: ~3 Liters
  • Inflation Time: ~600ms (Mechanical hardware delay)
  • Gas type: Compressed CO2
  • Construction Method: Sewn and Adhesive

Shown below are the two states of the airbag module: Inflated vs Not Inflated

Fluidic adaptor module specifications

The fluidic module is used to securely position several parts that are used to inflate the airbag module. The spring-loaded ratchet adaptor that is connected to the motor shaft is used to open the valve. This valve is used for sealing the CO2 cartridge. When the valve is opened, pressurized CO2 will be released to inflate the airbag.

  • Connected Parts: Motor, Spring, Ratchet Adapter, CO2 Cartridge
  • Material: PLA
  • Construction Method: 3D printing

DNA Sequencing

During my undergraduate years, I completed an engineering internship at Roche Sequencing Solutions, Santa Clara, California. As a member of the mechanical engineering team, I built software and hardware tools to support R&D on DNA sequencing systems. Here are the projects I found most interesting.

Role

Where

Built

Tools
Python with pandas, numpy and matplotlib

Pressure Sensor Implementation

My first task during the internship was to implement a pressure sensor into a DNA sequencing system. The objective was to utilize sensor data to detect when and where air entered the system during pump sequences.

I wrote a Python script to read, output, store and plot data of the pressure sensor in real time. The program utilizes Python libraries pandas, numpy and matplotlib to manage data, as well as displaying real-time animation of the readings when executed.

Shown here are the sensors; the sequencers they were fitted to are at the top of the page.

GUI for robot testing

To improve efficiency on test processes, a Python based GUI was created, which allowed test engineers to interact and control robot movements by simply clicking buttons. Before the creation of this program, test engineers had to execute movement commands through the command terminal, which was challenging for individuals who were not familiar with Linux and software programming. This program is also capable of executing sequential movements, as well as performing movements loops and saving movements sequences.

Fall Detection System

Arduino UNO + ProtoShield + MPU6050 + Enclosure

These renders were generated for a prototyping project proposal. The objective of the project was to design a fall detection system that utilizes a gyro-accelerometer sensor to detect human falling motion. The images show the early-stage design of the computational unit, which utilizes a fall detection algorithm written to an Arduino.

When a fall is detected, the system will output a signal to trigger a mechanism to release an airbag to protect the user's hip bone.
